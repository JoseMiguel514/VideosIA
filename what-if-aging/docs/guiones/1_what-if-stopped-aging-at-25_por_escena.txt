[hook1]
Imagine you blow out the candles on your twenty-fifth birthday... and your body just stops. Not your life. Your aging. Forever.

[hook2]
No gray hair. No wrinkles. No slowing down. Sounds like a dream. But here's the strange part. You'd still die. Just not the way you think.

[setup1]
Aging isn't one thing. Scientists describe about a dozen processes, called the hallmarks of aging, that slowly wear your body down.

[setup2]
DNA damage. Worn-out proteins. Cells that stop dividing, but refuse to die. Now imagine every one of them, on pause. What happens next?

[years]
For the first few years, nothing seems different. You're twenty-five. So are your friends. But at thirty-five, people start to notice.

[same]
Your skin still heals like new. Your joints never ache. Your heart beats just like it did at twenty-five. You don't look young for your age. You look exactly the same.

[telomeres]
Deep inside your body, something has changed. Normally, every time most of your cells divide, the protective caps on your chromosomes, called telomeres, get a little shorter. Eventually, the cell stops dividing for good. In your frozen body, that clock never runs down.

[jelly]
And nature has already tried something like this. A tiny jellyfish, Turritopsis dohrnii, can turn its adult body back into a juvenile stage. Again, and again. But here's the catch. It still gets eaten. It still gets sick. Not aging doesn't make you immortal.

[math]
So how long would you actually live? If you never aged, your risk of dying would stay at a twenty-five-year-old's level. And what kills twenty-five-year-olds? Mostly not disease. Accidents. Car crashes. Overdoses. Violence.

[lifespan]
Some rough estimates put your average lifespan at several hundred years. Maybe even close to a thousand. But that's an average. Not a promise.

[year200]
By your two hundredth birthday, you've said goodbye to everyone from your first life. More than once. You've had careers that no longer exist.

[risk]
And something shifts in your mind. Every car ride. Every ski trip. Every risk, now puts centuries of life on the line. Would you ever get on a motorcycle again?

[twist]
And here's the twist. For most of the biggest killers, like heart disease, many cancers, and Alzheimer's, the single greatest risk factor isn't smoking. It isn't diet. It's age itself.

[root]
That means scientists studying aging aren't just chasing youth. They may be chasing the root of many diseases at once.

[lab]
In animal studies, drugs that clear out zombie senescent cells have improved health in old mice. In humans, the research is still early. No one has stopped human aging. Not yet.

[outro1]
So maybe the real question isn't whether you'd want to stay twenty-five forever. It's what you'd do with all that time, knowing it could still end tomorrow.
